Lever 01 · Scout

How do agents find signals and drivers for scenarios?

A Scout agent continuously searches a search field defined by humans for signals, records each one with its evidence and groups related signals into themes. From those themes it proposes candidate drivers; humans make the final selection.

01 / Process

From search field to driver in five steps

  1. Define the search field

    Question, time horizon, industry and region determine which fields are scanned, for example along the STEEP dimensions: society, technology, economy, environment and politics.

  2. Scan sources

    The agent continuously reads specialist sources, public authorities, company announcements, research and the press within the search field, and filters out duplicates and noise.

  3. Record signals

    Every relevant observation is recorded as a signal with its source, date and a short description. No evidence, no signal.

  4. Cluster signals

    Related signals are grouped into themes. A theme backed by many independent sources carries different weight from a single hint.

  5. Propose drivers

    From the themes, the agent drafts candidate drivers: factors that will shape the future within the search field, each linked to the signals it rests on.

03 / Limits

What Scout cannot do

  • It only finds what leaves traces. Developments nobody is writing about yet remain invisible.
  • It inherits the bias of its sources. A search field built on a few types of source gives a distorted picture.
  • Frequency is not significance. A widely reported theme can be unimportant, a single signal decisive.
  • It does not answer questions nobody has asked. Without a well-defined search field it gathers material without direction.

04 / In practice

What this looks like in practice

Squawk continuously monitors technology, markets, politics, the environment and society, checks every observation against its source and condenses the results into an industry radar of themes with stage and trajectory, delivered weekly by email and monthly as a report (in German).
