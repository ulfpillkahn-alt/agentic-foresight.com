Lever 02 · Outlook

How do agents build scenarios by the book?

An Outlook agent takes the selected drivers step by step through the classic scenario process: interconnection, assessment by impact and uncertainty, key factors, scenario framework, write-up. At each junction a human confirms before the agent moves on.

01 / Process

From driver to scenario in seven steps

  1. Take over the drivers

    The starting point is the set of drivers a human selected from Scout's proposals, each with its evidence.

  2. Impact matrix

    Every driver is rated against every other: how strongly does A affect B? Row and column totals give the active and passive sums, which show which drivers push the system and which are pushed by it. The approach goes back to Frederic Vester's paper computer.

  3. Wilson matrix

    Drivers are placed by impact and uncertainty. High impact with low uncertainty belongs in every scenario; high impact with high uncertainty is what sets the scenarios apart.

  4. Select key factors

    Both matrices together point to a small number of key factors with high impact and an open outcome.

  5. Set up the scenario framework

    Possible states are described for each key factor and combined into consistent sets, for example as a scenario cross with two axes and four quadrants.

  6. Write out the scenarios

    For each quadrant the agent writes a coherent picture of the future, with its assumptions, its path and the signals that already point towards it today.

  7. Visualise

    The scenarios are turned into images, profiles or comparison boards so they can be laid side by side in workshops and boardrooms.

03 / Limits

What Outlook cannot do

  • It cannot produce a future that is not already present in its drivers. What Scout did not find is missing from the scenarios too.
  • Language models lean towards the probable. Without deliberate counter-steering, scenarios come out smoother and more alike than they should.
  • Consistency is not plausibility. A scenario free of contradictions can still miss reality.
  • Scenarios are not forecasts. The agent cannot provide a probability of occurrence beyond an estimate.

Methodological background:

  • Vester, Frederic: Die Kunst vernetzt zu denken. Ideen und Werkzeuge für einen neuen Umgang mit Komplexität. Stuttgart: Deutsche Verlags-Anstalt, 1999.
  • Gausemeier, Jürgen; Fink, Alexander; Schlake, Oliver: Szenario-Management. Planen und Führen mit Szenarien. München: Hanser, 1995.
  • Ralston, Bill; Wilson, Ian: The Scenario-Planning Handbook. Developing Strategies in Uncertain Times. Mason (Ohio): Thomson South-Western, 2006.
